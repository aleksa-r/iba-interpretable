# Pooled fitness, noise-aware replacement, ABC as an ensemble generator

## 0. Why

SPEC-2026-11-14-abc-v2 found that every search on the single-series fitness loses on rows it has
not used (−7 % to −16 % against the prior) and loses more the freer it is; only the colony of
the spread-start search, used as an ensemble, was level with the first ensemble and ahead of
group PCA in 4 of 18 tasks. The reading was that the limit is the fitness (four overlapping
78-week validation windows of one series), not the optimiser. Three directions were proposed and
the owner agreed to all three on 2026-11-14 ("ok I agree to all 3 continue"):
1. pooled fitness: one formula per asset class, scored on all its series at once;
2. noise-aware replacement: a candidate replaces a food source only if it is better by more than
   the standard error;
3. ABC as an ensemble generator only, with the spread start.

## 1. Questions and expectations (written before any result on real data)

Q1 Pooling: does a formula searched on the pooled fitness (asset class; whole frequency) gain on
   unused rows, pass the acceptance test more often than one in six, and beat the fixed prior E1
   out of sample?
Q2 Noise-aware replacement: does it reduce the fit to noise (inner gain) and the loss on unused
   rows, alone and together with pooling?
Q3 Ensembles: does any colony beat the first ensemble (B) or the spread-start one (`iba_wb`)? And
   does the SEARCH matter for the ensemble at all: are the starting colonies, averaged without any
   search, as good?
Q4 Does averaging an ensemble over its three seeds help?

Expected. From the synthetic check (section 4): pooling halves the inner gain and turns the gain
on unused rows positive EVEN WITHOUT ANY SIGNAL, because the pooled search drops literals and
groups (7–10 literals against 17) and fewer noisy regressors forecast better; so a gain of a
pooled arm over E1 on returns or tails is to be read as learned sparsity unless the arm also
beats the type's reference. The noise-aware rule slows the fit to noise but cannot stop it: the
colony holds many states and each comparison passes noise about one time in six. Real gains are
most likely for volatility, where there is signal. If the starting colonies do as well as the
searched ones, the ensemble result is about averaging perturbed formulas, not about ABC.

## 2. Data, cells, pools

Development series only. The 458 cells and 18 tasks of SPEC-2026-11-14-abc-v2 (every return and
volatility cell; tails at q = 10 %); protocol, labels and estimators as SPEC-2026-11-13. Within a
frequency and task all series share their rows, so the selection folds of a block are the same
rows for every series. Pools (`ibanext.protocol.study.pool_units`): **C** each asset class
(weekly: 8 classes with 5, 8, 2, 8, 5, 3, 5 and 1 series; monthly: the 11 hedge-fund indices);
**F** every development series of the frequency (37 weekly; monthly F = C). Forecasts of E1, B,
group PCA and the references come from the first study, those of the per-series greedy searches
from SPEC-2026-11-14-abc-v2 (same cells, dates and labels; the report checks that).

## 3. The arms (`ibanext.search.pooled`)

Twelve arms = fitness data {S one series, C asset class, F frequency} × replacement {greedy,
noise-aware} × start {anchored, spread}. Two exist: S greedy anchored = the first ABC with the
clean test (`iba_ac`, `iba_ar`, B), S greedy spread = W (`iba_w`, `iba_wr`, `iba_wb`). Ten are new:
`iba_sna`, `iba_snw`, `iba_ca`, `iba_cw`, `iba_cna`, `iba_cnw`, `iba_fa`, `iba_fw`, `iba_fna`,
`iba_fnw`. Same for all: crisp formulas, at most 3 literals per group, budget 2 000 distinct
formulas per block, colony 20, abandonment after 10 failures, 3 seeds, linear estimator.

- **Start.** Anchored: the first study's space and colony (the prior and its one-move neighbours,
  at most 4 status changes from the prior, scouts next to the prior). Spread: W's (the prior and
  19 random formulas, no distance bound, scouts at random formulas).
- **Pooled fitness.** One formula for the pool, in generic column names (the target's own columns
  are `__target___…`) over the union of the members' columns; a member that lacks a column (the
  five targets whose own factor is excluded) drops that literal, exactly as its prior is built, so
  the pooled prior is every member's own prior. Each member keeps its own linear model (ridge
  α = 1 / logistic C = 1 in the fitness, tuned in the forecasts). Every loss is divided by the
  member's fitness-fold loss under the prior:
  fitness = mean over members of [fitness-fold loss / the prior's] + 0.005 per literal in excess
  of the prior. With one member this orders formulas exactly as the first fitness (tested).
- **Greedy replacement** (as before): a candidate replaces its source if its fitness is lower; the
  winner is the best fitness ever evaluated.
- **Noise-aware replacement.** The profile of a formula is its normalised pointwise loss on the
  validation rows of the fitness folds, averaged over the folds that contain the row and over the
  members. A candidate replaces its source only if the mean of (source profile − candidate
  profile), less the change in the literal term, exceeds one standard error of non-overlapping
  block means (blocks of max(h, 4) rows): the acceptance rule applied inside the search. The
  winner is the best-fitness state the colony has held that passes the same test against the
  prior; if none does, the prior. (The proposal said "fold-to-fold standard error"; four
  overlapping folds do not give a usable one, so the standard error is taken over dates.)
- **Acceptance test** (all arms): the one-standard-error rule on the CLEAN outer rows, for pooled
  arms on the per-date mean over members of the normalised loss difference to the prior. One
  decision per pool, block and seed; a member whose pool's winner is not accepted keeps its prior.
- **Forecasts.** Stage 5 (`scripts/run_pooled_search.py`) runs the pooled searches on rows
  admissible at each block origin and stores winner, test result and colony. Stage 6 (the engine)
  turns them into forecasts per series, and runs the per-series arms. Readings per arm and seed:
  `<m>|lin` tested winner or prior, `<m>_r|lin` winner without the test, `<m>_b` colony ensemble.
- **Controls** (direction 3): `iba_rnda` and `iba_rndw`, the STARTING colonies of the anchored and
  of the spread search as ensembles — no search, no fitness.
- **Derived in the report:** every ensemble averaged over its three seeds (`@3`).

32 new configurations (10 arms × 3 readings, 2 controls); 14 seed-averaged ensembles.

Pooling works as intended on the noise (half the inner gain, positive gain on unused rows), and
the gain without signal shows what it is made of: sparser formulas. Twelve searches per cell of
the table; the differences between neighbouring cells are not established.

## 6. Statistics, reporting and the decision rule (`scripts/make_abc3_report.py`, written before the run)

1. Per arm and target type: gain of the winner on the fitness folds and on the clean rows, share
   of winners passing the test, share of winners that differ from the prior, literals.
2. Per (frequency, task), all development series of the frequency pooled on the same dates, paired
   date-block-bootstrap 95 % intervals (2 000 draws, block ≥ h) of the relative difference in the
   primary loss: every arm's three readings against E1; every ensemble and both controls against
   B and against group PCA; the controls also against `iba_wb`; seed-averaged ensembles against
   E1 and group PCA; tested winners and ensembles against the type's reference.
3. The three factors, each as paired contrasts at fixed levels of the other two (C against S, F
   against S; noise-aware against greedy; spread against anchored), for each reading.
4. SPA per task: does any of the 32 new configurations beat E1, B, `iba_wb`, group PCA, the
   reference. Seed spread of the ensembles.
5. Decision rule, applied mechanically, as in SPEC-2026-11-14-abc-v2. A candidate (a new arm's
   tested winner against E1; a new arm's or a control's ensemble against B) QUALIFIES if it is
   significantly better than its incumbent in more tasks than it is significantly worse AND its
   mean loss is lower in at least 3 of the 4 target types. Among several, the lowest mean relative
   loss with the types weighted equally. If none qualifies, E1 and the earlier ensembles stay.
   A qualifying pooled arm is then read against the reference (section 1) before any claim.

## 7. Compute

search: 0.13–0.15 ms per formula and member for returns and volatility, 0.55–0.6 ms for tails),

(`scripts/run_abc3.sh`: stage 5 twice, stage 6 twice, the second pass retries failures, then the
report), started on Saturday 14 November.
